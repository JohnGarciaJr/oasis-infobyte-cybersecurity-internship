# Social Engineering Report

## Overview
Social engineering is the manipulation of individuals into performing actions or revealing confidential information. Because it targets human behavior rather than technical vulnerabilities, it remains one of the most effective attack vectors.

## Common Social Engineering Techniques

### Phishing
Deceptive emails designed to steal credentials or deliver malware.

### Vishing
Voice-based attacks where attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations.

### Smishing
SMS-based phishing that encourages users to click malicious links.

### Pretexting
Attackers create a fabricated scenario to gain trust and extract information.

### Tailgating
Gaining physical access by following an authorized person into a restricted area.

## Psychological Principles Exploited
- **Authority:** “I’m from IT, I need your login.”  
- **Urgency:** “Your account will be locked in 10 minutes.”  
- **Scarcity:** “Limited-time offer — click now.”  
- **Trust:** Impersonating coworkers or leadership.  
- **Fear:** Threats of account suspension or legal action.  

## Real‑World Examples
- Fake password reset emails  
- CEO fraud / Business Email Compromise (BEC)  
- Fake tech support calls requesting remote access  

## Prevention & Defense
- Conduct regular employee awareness training  
- Implement MFA to reduce credential theft impact  
- Use email filtering and anti-phishing tools  
- Establish verification procedures for financial or sensitive requests  
- Encourage a strong reporting culture  

## Conclusion
Because humans are often the weakest link, continuous training and strong verification processes are critical to reducing social engineering risk.
